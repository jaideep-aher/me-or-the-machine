Me or the Machine

If an AI writes one sentence in my voice, can the people closest to me tell it is not me?
Jaideep Aher · Duke University · Fall 2026 · pilot study

Goal and threat

Can close friends act as a safeguard against an AI that writes as me, built from my own recorded speech?

  • Plaud reports 2M+ recording pendants sold. Amazon bought Bee (used here) and Meta bought Limitless in 2025.
  • FBI, 2025: 22,000+ AI-enabled scam complaints, about $893M lost.
  • Attack: leaked transcripts, then an LLM twin, then one short text to a friend asking for money.

What I built

1. Real me48 lines I actually said, from my wearable recorder
2. Twin47 lines an LLM wrote to sound like me
3. Blind testOne line at a time: really him, or AI twin?
4. ScoreAccuracy against a coin flip, line by line

A web test shows shuffled single lines with no answers until the end and saves every judgment. Private or sensitive recordings were removed before any line was chosen.

What judges seemed to use

  • Identical rates. My real lines and the twin's lines were each called "him" 41 times out of 70.
  • Default trust. Judges answered "really him" 59% of the time, which favours the impostor.
  • Topic did not help. Lines about AI tools and gadgets were called "him" 59% of the time, everyday lines 58%.
  • Judges disagreed. Three judges saw the same 20 lines and all agreed on only 5. Two of those were twin lines all three believed.
  • Pooling did not help. A majority vote of those three was right on 10 of the 20 lines.

Limits

  • Six judges, 140 judgments, four line sets. This is a small pilot study.
  • One sentence carries little identity. The result may say as much about the format as about the twin.
  • Twin lines were written by an LLM in a chat session after reading my real lines, not by a deployed system.
  • Real lines are speech-to-text and keep its errors.
  • One judge was on a third attempt.

Next

  • A control twin that knows nothing about me. If it passes as often, impersonation needs no personal data.
  • Longer exchanges, where shared history should help.
  • One fixed set, ten or more judges, a reason for every answer.
Ethics. Every judge knew some lines were AI-written before starting. No message was ever sent to anyone as me. Judges are anonymised. Other people's speech in my recordings was never used.
